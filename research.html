01 / Semidefinite optimization

On exactness of SDP relaxation for the maximum cut problem

Joint work with Avinash Bhardwaj, Vishnu Narayanan, and Abhishek Pathapati.

Preprint · arXiv ↗

Abstract · condensed

This work identifies graph classes for which the semidefinite relaxation of the maximum cut problem is exact and establishes conditions for uniqueness of the SDP optimum. Two graph operations preserve solution rank and exactness, showing how a small structural core can govern exactness in arbitrarily large graphs. The paper also resolves two questions posed by Mirka and Williamson about the relationship between uniqueness of a maximum cut and uniqueness of the SDP optimum, and about optimal SDP solutions outside the convex hull of rank-one solutions.

02 / Features & balancing

Max-k-Cut via Node Features

Joint work with Vishnu Narayanan and Avinash Bhardwaj.

Preprint · arXiv ↗

Abstract · condensed

We study Max-k-Cut when edge weights arise as inner products of node-feature vectors. A normal-cone argument gives a sufficient condition for exactness of the Frieze–Jerrum relaxation, covering perfect feature balance and feature dominance. The objective can be expressed through the squared norms of aggregate feature vectors in each part, connecting graph partitioning to vector balancing. A greedy feature-balancing algorithm retains the classical 1 − 1/k approximation guarantee and is optimal under feature dominance. For nonnegative rank-one feature graphs, classical load-balancing bounds provide a computable a posteriori optimality-gap certificate.

03 / Algorithmic game theory

Social Optimality Does Not Imply Coalitional Stability in Unweighted Max-k-Cut Games

Hritiz Gogoi

Preprint · arXiv ↗

Abstract · condensed

For every k ≥ 3, this paper constructs a finite, simple, connected, unweighted graph with a socially optimal coloring that is not a strong equilibrium. A coalition can make every member strictly better off while moving to another optimal coloring, leaving total social welfare unchanged and imposing losses on players outside the coalition. An identity connects coalitional gains, changes in cut value, and internal conflicts. It recovers the stability guarantee for optimal colorings at maximum degree at most 2k − 1 and yields a sharp bound on remaining internal monochromatic edges after strong deviations from general Nash equilibria.

04 / Applied statistics

Lagged Effects of Rainfall on Malaria: A Case Study of Meghalaya

Joint work with Holendro Singh Chungkham.

Indian Journal of Public Health · 69(1), 57–62 · 2025

Publication · DOI ↗

Abstract · condensed

This study examines how rainfall at different monthly lags interacts in its association with malaria incidence. Monthly malaria records and climatic data from four Meghalaya districts during 2005–2017 are analyzed using a varying-coefficient distributed-lag nonlinear model. Rainfall in the preceding month changes the relationship between malaria incidence and rainfall at longer lags: low preceding-month rainfall can accompany increasing incidence as rainfall rises, while high preceding-month rainfall can accompany a decreasing pattern. These findings emphasize the importance of interactions between lagged exposures in understanding and predicting malaria transmission.
